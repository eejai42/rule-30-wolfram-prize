Abstract
We introduce and formally motivate the Business Rule Completeness Conjecture (BRCC). It
posits that all declarative semantics for any conceptual model—whether an app, workflow,
or other domain—can be unambiguously captured using only five core primitives: Data
in an ACID-compliant Schema that supports Lookups, Rollups (Aggregations), and
Calculated Fields, treating time as just another dimension. We present a theoretical
proof sketch, inspired by Turing-completeness arguments, showing that these five primitives
are sufficient to encode any finite, computable business rule. Thus, no external syntax or
formalism is fundamentally necessary to express the rulebook (the declarative what), apart
from an imperative engine that executes these definitions (the how ).


1     Concrete Applications and Implications
1.1    Minimizing Ripple Effects and Model Co-Evolution
A major source of complexity in modern software or Model-Driven Engineering (MDE) arises
when the conceptual schema (metamodel, M2) changes, causing ripple effects through the
lower-level models (M1) and code or data structures (M0). Under BRCC, however, the
metamodel and model are fundamentally locked together as two sides of the same coin in a
single database structure. When the M2 level changes, the M1 level updates atomically and
consistently, thanks to the ACID properties. This mitigates the typical model repair process,
in which partial updates or version mismatches cause the system to drift out of sync.

Model Repair and ACID. Because BRCC systems reside in ACID-compliant data
stores, the model and metamodel remain valid as a single transaction. There is no par-
tial state in which the new M2 is defined but the M1 hasnt caught up. Both are stored
consistently in the same environment, so no separate transformer steps are needed to keep
them aligned. By construction, each instance record and its schema maintain coherence.

1.2    Reusable Transformations and Tooling
BRCC is domain-agnostic, so general-purpose transformations become possible. Because the
entire environment (including metamodel definitions and instance data) is stored in a single
place, one can build or reuse transformations without domain-specific code. For instance:

    • ssotme:// Protocol and CLI: An open-source mechanism akin to git for business
      rules that allows version control of all domain definitions (metamodel) and instance
      data (model) in sync.

    • Airtable-to-Django, Baserow-to-DotNet, etc.: Over 300 free or open-source tools
      demonstrate how changes at the conceptual (S, D, L, A, F) level automatically flow
      into domain-specific environments.

1.3    Falsifiability in Practice
BRCC is explicitly falsifiable. To invalidate it, one need only produce a business rule that
can be:

  1. Expressed in natural language or a standard programming environment,

  2. Yet cannot be represented using Schema, Data, Lookups, Aggregations, and Calculated
     Fields under ACID.

Decades of experience (and attempts at counterexamples) have thus far not yielded any such
rule. This practice-based evidence aligns with the formal argument in Section 4: once the
system can simulate universal computation, it suffices for capturing all finite, computable
business rules. In short, BRCC remains standing, practically and theoretically, until someone
finds a concrete counterexample.

2     Introduction
2.1     The Business Rule Completeness Conjecture (BRCC)
The conjecture states:

      “The declarative semantics for any conceptual model (app or otherwise) can
      be unambiguously captured with just 5 simple primitives: data, in an ACID-
      compliant schema that supports lookups, rollups, and calculated fields. Time is
      treated as just another dimension, and the core difference is between the rulebook
      (the declarative what) versus the runtime engine (the imperative how).”

    In other words, if you have:

    1. Schema (S): A structure of tables/fields in which your data resides.

    2. Data (D): The actual records populating these tables (the facts).

    3. Lookups (L): References (foreign keys) that connect rows and form relationships.

    4. Aggregations (A) (or rollups): Summaries of data over groups (count, sum, etc.).

    5. Calculated Fields (F): Formulas or expressions that derive new values from existing
       data.

plus ACID compliance (atomic, consistent, isolated, and durable transactions), then any
business rule—no matter how sophisticated—can be defined within that environment, with-
out resorting to external syntaxes or specialized logic languages.

2.2     Motivation
This conjecture challenges the notion that complex business logic necessarily requires special-
ized rule engines or domain-specific languages. Instead, it hypothesizes that the fundamental
building blocks inherent to many modern databases (e.g., reference/lookup fields, formula
fields, rollups) suffice to capture all finite, computable rules. From a computer science per-
spective, business rules are ultimately just functions or predicates over finite data, which in
turn are governed by standard computability theory.

2.3     Related Work
The conceptual roots trace back to Turing [1] and Church [2], who established that any
effectively computable function can be simulated by a universal model of computation. In
the database realm, Codds relational model [3] introduced the concept of schemas, data,
and relational integrity constraints, which form the foundation for modern ACID-compliant
systems.

3      Statement of the Conjecture
BRCC:

       For any finite, computable business rule r, there exists an encoding of r as a
       combination of (S, D, L, A, F ) in a single ACID-compliant datastore, such that
       the rule is captured unambiguously without external syntactic artifacts.

3.1     Falsifiability
To falsify BRCC, one must exhibit a finite, computable rule r that cannot be realized purely
within the five primitives. Because of the bridging to Turing-completeness, this would require
r to be a computable function that somehow defies standard universal constructions—an
unlikely scenario under current computability theory.


4      Proof Sketch of Turing-Completeness
4.1     Business Rules as Computable Functions
A business rule is any finite statement about how system outputs or constraints should
be determined from system inputs. Computability theory tells us such rules correspond to
partial or total recursive functions over finite data structures.

4.2     Representing a Universal Model in (S, D, L, A, F )
Let us treat (S, D, L, A, F ) within an ACID datastore as follows:

    1. Schema (S) and Data (D) store the tape or state of a Turing machine (or an equiv-
       alent universal computational model).

    2. Lookups (L) connect rows that represent consecutive tape cells or transitions.

    3. Aggregations (A) summarize and group data, enabling us to handle repeated struc-
       ture or count transitions.

    4. Calculated Fields (F) apply formula-based operations to combine data from multiple
       lookups, effectively executing the universal transition function (e.g., If current state is
       Q1 and tape symbol is 0, write 1 and move to Q2).

By appropriately structuring these schema objects and formulas, we can reproduce each step
of a universal computation. ACID ensures that intermediate steps remain coherent (atomic,
consistent) and that references are not broken (isolation, durability).

4.3    Time and Event-Driven Logic
Many business rules rely on the last 30 days or status changed from X to Y. Under the
conjecture, these are addressed by storing timestamps or events as rows in Data (S+D),
referencing them via Lookups, filtering or grouping with Aggregations, and deriving time-
based logic in Calculated Fields. Thus, no additional temporal syntax is needed; time is just
another dimension in the data model.


5     Discussion and Practical Caveats
Unbounded Formulas. The Turing-completeness argument often requires potentially
unbounded formula complexity and iterative referencing. Real systems (e.g., many no-
code/low-code platforms) may cap recursion depth or formula length, thereby restricting
pure completeness.
Execution vs. Declaration. BRCC emphasizes the declarative dimension—what the rule
means. The imperative dimension of how the system executes or optimizes that logic is a
separate layer (the runtime engine).
Formalization. A full formal proof would specify the exact syntax and semantics for
(S, D, L, A, F ) and walk through how to simulate each step of a known universal computing
mechanism. The above sections provide a comprehensive sketch sufficient for conceptual
acceptance.


6     Conclusion
We have stated and motivated the Business Rule Completeness Conjecture (BRCC),
asserting that five core primitives in an ACID datastore—Schema, Data, Lookups, Aggre-
gations, and Calculated Fields—can encode any finite, computable business rule. A Turing-
completeness style argument underscores why no additional syntactic formalism is strictly
necessary to capture the entire rulebook of a system. By storing everything in a single
consistent schema, we ensure that the logic remains unambiguous and self-contained.
   Hence, BRCC stands as a theoretically well-founded claim, awaiting a counterexample—
which standard computability theory indicates should not exist—to falsify it.
